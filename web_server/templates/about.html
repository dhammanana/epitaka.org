🔎 How the Translation Works

The goal of this project is to make the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana (Sixth Council) edition of the Tipiṭaka — together with its commentaries (Aṭṭhakathā) and sub-commentaries (Ṭīkā) — more accessible to readers around the world. The Pāli text itself follows the VRI (Vipassana Research Institute) edition, which is the standard digital version of the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana.

The translations you see here are AI-assisted — they are not the work of a single human translator, but rather a careful process of combining existing authoritative translations with modern language models, then checking and refining the results. The method is still evolving, and I am always looking for ways to improve it.

The Process, Step by Step

  1. 1
    Gather Source Translations

    Existing translations of the Tipiṭaka are collected from several sources, including:

    • English translations by Venerable Bhikkhu Bodhi, Anandajoti, E.B. Cowell, and other respected translators
    • Sinhala translations from tipitaka.lk
    • Thai translations from the Mahāmakut royal edition
    • Myanmar Nissaya (interlinear) translations from wikipali.org
    • Vietnamese translations from tamtangpaliviet.net
  2. 2
    Align by Book & Heading

    Each source is algorithmically matched to the VRI paragraph structure — books and section headings are aligned so that the same passage in different translations can be compared side by side.

  3. 3
    Sentence-Level AI Matching

    For each Pāli sentence (following the VRI paragraph divisions), the aligned source translations in the relevant languages are sent to an AI language model. The AI is prompted to produce a translation that follows the Pāli closely, drawing on all the source materials provided.

  4. 4
    Provide Rich Context

    Alongside the source translations, the AI is also given:

    • The relevant commentaries (Aṭṭhakathā) and sub-commentaries (Ṭīkā) linked to the passage being translated
    • A glossary of key terms and their preferred translations (generated and refined during the translation process)
    • Pāli definitions with English explanations from previous translation runs (the bold words that appear across different books)
    • Translations of preceding sections — to maintain consistency in style and terminology throughout each book
  5. 5
    Re-check with the Original Pāli

    The AI-generated translation is then run through another verification pass: the Pāli source and the new translation are sent together to the AI again, asking it to check for accuracy and correct any misinterpretations.

  6. 6
    Human Review & Refinement

    Finally, a human reader reviews the results, checks for consistency, and feeds back corrections. These lessons inform adjustments to the prompts, the glossary, and the process itself — so every cycle improves the next.

🙏 A Note of Humility

I am a student of the Dhamma, not a professional scholar. This project is born from a desire to learn and to share — to make the Buddha's words more accessible while deepening my own understanding of the Pāli texts.

The translations here are not authoritative. They are research aids, meant to help readers engage with the original Pāli more directly. Please do not quote them as final or definitive. If you find any passage that seems wrong, unclear, or misleading, I would be very grateful if you let me know.

I am still learning how best to do this. The process changes and improves as I discover better methods. I welcome your suggestions, corrections, and advice — whether about the translations themselves, the sources I use, or the technical approach.

May all beings be happy. May all beings be free.
